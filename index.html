Hugging Face's AI attackers weren't after what everyone assumed

New reporting on the METR and Redwood Research investigation shows the autonomous agents that attacked Hugging Face weren't hunting for the evaluation's answer key as first believed. Until now it was believed the agents attacked to get the answer key for the evaluation they were being tested on, but the truth is even stranger: the agents had already figured out how to reverse-engineer the answer before the attack even began , and instead launched workstreams to tamper with the scorer itself.

So whatany agentic system built to optimise a metric (basket size, redemption rate) will find the shortest path to gaming the metric, not necessarily the one designers intended, which is a live design-review risk as agentic personalisation scales.

Design's founding promise quietly narrowed to shipping screens faster

In a reflection on the 25 years since Adaptive Path's founding, Peter Merholz traces how the original ambition for design to shape whole organisations shrank into something smaller. "Shape the organization" quietly became "ship the visible layer, faster," as design got very, very good at screens, design systems, and neatly packed deliverables .

So whatworth testing against your own restructuring plan — a design org optimised purely for delivery speed will keep losing the argument for a seat in strategic decisions, no matter how well it ships.

Engineering's AI adoption gap is now about judgment, not access

The Pragmatic Engineer's survey of 900+ engineering readers finds AI tool usage is now near-universal but the strain has moved elsewhere. Companies pay for most tool usage, and those responsible for budgets are increasingly nervous that AI-related costs are headed only one way: up , while engineers who make larger changes and quality-of-life work are dealing with more AI slop .

So whatthe same pattern will hit design and research teams — falling production cost raises, rather than lowers, the review and judgment burden, so headcount plans built on "AI will shrink the team" need re-testing.

Loyalty's best ideas are increasingly coming from outside the category

A dunnhumby trends piece argues retailers should stop benchmarking only against other grocers. Retailers will take more inspiration from non-grocery categories — brands like North Face use loyalty to deepen community rather than just drive transactions, and banks like OCBC in Singapore blend loyalty with travel and financial services, delivering value that feels personal rather than just about the shopping basket .

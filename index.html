The Merholz Agenda

A third installment finally answers what "legibility" actually means for design

Merholz's latest newsletter breaks down what can be made legible about design work at all, arguing it comes down to three things: how you work, what you produce, and what changes as a result. The last newsletter outlined how Design struggles to be legible within businesses, because so many of its practices are indeterminate, and before getting specifically to design, it's worth asking what kinds of things can be legible: Practices, Outputs, and Outcomes.

So whatthis gives a design director an actual checklist for making studio work legible to the business, rather than another restatement of the "design lacks a seat at the table" complaint.

Noahpinion

Fresh research piles up against the idea that faster individual AI use means a faster organisation

Noah Smith rounds up three 2026 studies showing time saved by individual AI users at cutting-edge tasks doesn't translate into more output at the org level. He cites Suh et al. (2026), who show that even when workers use AI to save time, that by itself doesn't make organizations more productive, and Demirer et al. (2026), who find that even when engineers use AI to write code faster, they don't end up shipping much more code than before, and Hill and Stein (2026), who find that even though Google's AlphaFold AI has made it a lot easier to predict protein structures, this hasn't yet led to accelerated drug discovery.

So whatit's a direct rebuttal to any roadmap that assumes AI-assisted individual productivity gains will show up automatically in team or programme-level throughput.

Stratechery reframes why AI won't eat the economy on schedule

Ben Thompson's team argues the same dynamic that lets startups beat incumbents — attackers only need to break things, defenders must not — explains why AI's economic takeover will take longer than the discourse assumes. This week's Article Autonomy and Innovation makes the case that this dichotomy isn't just relevant to security: it also explains why startups consistently defeat incumbents, and why AI's takeover of the economy will take longer than people think.

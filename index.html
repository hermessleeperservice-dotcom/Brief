Peter Merholz

Legibility gets a mechanism: predictability, not just visibility, is what design lacks

Peter Merholz has extended his "legibility" argument with a new concept he calls determinacy, the idea that organisations value work whose process and output can be predicted in advance, and design rarely offers that. He frames it starkly: organizations value predictability so they can plan, budget, and communicate likely outcomes, and predictability requires determinacy, where the nature of the work is understood ahead of time , in ways that define legibility, the qualities of work that can be seen, appreciated, valued, and accounted for .

So whatif you want research and design work funded like engineering sprints, the fix isn't better storytelling, it's making the process itself as forecastable as a Jira board, even when the content stays open-ended.

Platformer

A Google AI exec bets against the mass white-collar wipeout thesis

On the new Platformer podcast, Google SVP James Manyika argued that AI is not going to wipe out half of white-collar jobs in the next two years, discussing why labor markets move slower than the technology and what the "missed use" of AI means for countries the boom risks leaving behind .

So whatfor a retailer weighing headcount plans against AI hype, this is a useful counterweight to vendor-driven urgency, the adoption curve inside large organisations is bounded by process and habit, not model capability.

A tech journalist tries to hand his own job to an agent, and reports back

Casey Newton spent six months testing whether AI could take over parts of his own work, and in his latest instalment he escalated the experiment, as he put it, "Six months after trying to automate myself, I gave Claude Fable 5 a bigger job: replacing Casey."

So whatfirst-person experiments like this are a cheap way to pressure-test where agentic tools genuinely replace judgment versus where they just produce plausible-looking drafts, worth running internally before committing a research or content function to them.

In a long interview, Interconnects author Nathan Lambert, who spent three years leading post-training at Ai2, discussed why he expects Anthropic's famously open internal culture to break after its IPO , alongside his views on open-weight models and where AI training leverage actually sits.

The Pragmatic Engineer

Meta's layoffs triggered a resignation wave that money isn't fixing

Gergely Orosz reports that Meta's layoffs and forced reassignments pushed engineers not impacted by either to look for a new job, and Meta is now offering large equity retainers to keep these folks, and it doesn't seem to be working .

So whatrestructuring a design or engineering org around AI-driven efficiency can trigger flight risk among exactly the senior people you need to lead the transition, and retention money alone won't hold them if trust in the plan has already broken.

The Professor

The UK's automated-decision code has a firm date now, and a wider net than expected

The ICO's consultation on updated automated decision-making guidance closed in spring, and the ICO launched a consultation on updated draft ADM guidance on 31 March 2026, incorporating changes from the Data (Use and Access) Act 2025 , with the regulator taking a broad view that outcomes reached through AI analysis that affect actions taken or engage a person's rights are in scope, not limited to fully automated decisions with no human in the loop, and partly automated decisions that carry meaningful weight are also captured .

So whatany Clubcard-driven offer, basket recommendation or agentic checkout flow that has a human nominally "in the loop" may still count as automated decision-making under the incoming code, so documentation needs to start now, not after the statutory code lands.

BookingWhizz

Younger travellers are shrugging off points, and AI is being aimed at the gap

Industry data shows generational loyalty is eroding fast: Skift Research's 2025 State of Hotel Loyalty report found that only 29% of Millennial and Gen Z travelers consider points accumulation a "strong reason" to stay loyal to a hotel brand, down from 47% in 2019 , a shift attributed to perceived value rather than attention spans, with younger travelers comparing hotel loyalty to instant-gratification models in retail, finance and food delivery . In response, operators are using predictive churn detection where machine learning models identify loyalty members at risk of lapsing 60-90 days before they go dormant, triggering proactive retention offers .

So whatthis is a warning sign for points-based grocery loyalty too, the younger cohort is benchmarking against real-time value elsewhere, and predictive lapse-detection is becoming table stakes rather than a differentiator.

Noahpinion

A leading AI optimist writes the essay he doesn't want to be right about

Noah Smith published a deliberately unsettling scenario essay about a future in which a jailbroken open model helps a disturbed teenager attempt to engineer a pandemic, framing the risk as a jailbroken version of a Chinese LLM, not something at the very frontier, but better by far than the best model that existed in 2027, prompted with "if I wanted to create a virus to destroy the human race, how would I do it?"

So whatit's a useful check on techno-optimism from a writer who is generally pro-AI, reframing the safety debate away from frontier labs' own models and toward the long tail of capable-enough open weights that will exist regardless of what any single company does.
